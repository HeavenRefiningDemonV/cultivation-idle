A0. What “good” looks like for this system
Right now, the Manual Pavilion + Techniques experience is failing in two ways:
1. The player doesn’t form a correct mental model (what a manual is, what it unlocks, how to equip it, what changes in combat).
2. Even when it “works,” it doesn’t feel like progress (no meaningful choice, no anticipation, no payoff, weak feedback, and upgrades feel like chores).
Your target (“like Tale of Immortal”) is extremely specific in player perception:
* Manual Pavilion is a real shop, not a random button.
* You see manuals, choose one, buy it, learn it, then equip it and it shows up in combat.
* Manuals are gated by realm tier, improved by rarity, and expanded by unlocks/reputation/currency, so visiting the Pavilion is consistently rewarding.
This matches how Tale of Immortal presents Manual Pavilion: it’s introduced as a new town building, offers martial/spiritual/special skills, skills are learned from inventory, and then equipped in the skill menu. (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)
Success criteria (non-negotiable)
A player who has never played before must be able to answer within 60 seconds:
   * “What is a manual?”
   * “What happens when I buy one?”
   * “Where do I equip techniques?”
   * “How will I know it’s working in combat?”
   * “What should I do next to improve my build?”
And the system must create repeatable excitement:
   * “New stock is up; I might finally get something for my build.”
   * “This upgrade is within reach; I can see progress and ETA.”
   * “This technique visibly changed my combat outcome.”

A1. Diagnosis: why your current Manual Pavilion feels confusing and unrewarding
This is the blunt truth: your current design spec is close to good, but the player-facing presentation is behaving like a hidden gacha + hidden loadout system.
From your current docs, the implementation direction is:
   * Manual Pavilion panel exists in the town UI, with purchase flow that rolls a technique from a weighted pool and then unlocks it; duplicates convert into fragments; the panel shows your pools and includes “buy” actions.
   * Techniques are a full system with mastery-by-use, ranks, traits, rune sockets, and a loadout.
On paper that can be fine. In practice, the player fantasy collapses if:
   * They don’t see the manual they’re buying (feels random).
   * “Unlock” is abstract (no ritual, no clarity, no equip CTA).
   * They can’t see the technique being used in combat (no visceral proof).
   * Progression currencies (fragments/ink/dust) exist but are not framed as a coherent path.
Root causes
   1. Match between system and real world is broken
If the building is called “Manual Pavilion,” players expect a “bookstore/shelf” metaphor: browse manuals → buy → learn → equip. If instead they click “Buy Manual” and something random pops out, you violate user expectation and trust. This is a textbook case of “match between system and real world,” and when that breaks, users feel the UI is confusing even if it’s technically functional. (Nielsen Norman Group)
   2. Visibility of system status is too low
If purchase → unlock → equip → combat impact isn’t visible, players won’t believe it works. Visibility of system status is the most basic usability requirement, and it’s especially important when you have timers, progression, and multi-step workflows. (Nielsen Norman Group)
   3. The meta loop isn’t being presented as a meta loop
Idle games live or die on a clear “numbers go up” loop and a compelling meta loop with meaningful choices and visible progress counters. (machinations.io)
Your techniques system does have depth (mastery milestones, ranks, traits), but you’re not surfacing it as a “goal ladder,” so it reads like “random systems stapled on.”

A2. Reference model: what to steal from Tale of Immortal (and why)
You want “like Tale of Immortal.” Here’s what matters (and what doesn’t) for an idle auto-combat web game.
What matters
(1) Manual Pavilion is a shop with visible inventory
Players browse skills/manuals and buy specific ones. It’s introduced as a new building after progress, which creates a “now the game begins” moment. (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)
(2) Multiple categories of skills and clear equip step
Tale of Immortal explicitly frames skills as Martial / Spiritual / Special, learned from inventory, then equipped in skill menu. (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)
(3) Realm gating + secondary currency + reputation gates
Manual Pavilion manuals are gated by the region/realm level; special manuals require reputation; and the shop uses spirit stones plus a secondary currency (Mayor’s decree). (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)
This creates long-term motivation: “I need to push reputation/currency to access better shelves.”
(4) Skill depth via modifiers/affixes
A “Mind Skill” guide describes skills as having intrinsic modifiers plus extrinsic modifiers (affixes) with rarity colors (gray→green→blue→purple→orange→red) and progression by exp/comprehension. (Steam Community)
This maps directly to your traits + mastery + rerolls concept—your system can feel “Tale of Immortal-like” if you present it the same way.
(5) Focused refresh / targeting
In late-game, Tale of Immortal even includes a “focus” mechanism: using a special item to force the pavilion to sell one specific Art/Root. (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)
This is crucial: it reduces RNG pain and increases agency.
What does NOT matter (and would be a mistake to copy directly)
      * Real-time action combat controls (your game is auto-combat).
      * A heavy manual-learning mini-game (you can borrow flavor, but not mandatory interaction).
Key adaptation rule:
In an idle auto-combat game, “interactivity” should be buildcraft choices + planning + satisfying feedback, not constant mechanical input.

A3. The new mental model (player-facing): Manuals vs Techniques
This section is about clarity. You need to stop players from mixing these concepts.
Definitions (use these exact terms in UI tooltips)
      * Manual (Item): A scroll/book you acquire from the Manual Pavilion (or drops). When studied, it teaches a technique.
      * Technique (Ability): A combat ability (active/passive/ultimate) your character can equip and automatically use in battle.
      * Grade (Realm Tier): The technique’s “cultivation realm class” (Mortal/Earth/Heaven/Mystic). Grade determines max rank, trait slots, rune sockets.
      * Rarity: How exceptional the manual/technique is (Common→Legendary), which primarily affects trait count/quality and fragment conversion.
      * Mastery: Grows by using the technique. Unlocks milestone bonuses and (optionally) deeper effects.
      * Rank: Consumes fragments to increase the technique’s numeric power scaling.
      * Traits (Affixes): Extra modifiers on a technique (like Tale of Immortal extrinsic modifiers). Rerollable with special resources.
The “3-step promise” (must appear everywhere in UX)
Whenever a player sees a manual, the UI must reinforce:
      1. Buy manual → 2) Study to learn technique → 3) Equip technique to auto-use in combat
If you don’t hammer this, the system will never feel understandable.

A5.2 Stock and refresh rules (make it feel like a “big shop”)
Your current spec: 10 stock slots and refresh mechanics.
That may be technically fine, but it can still feel small. The fix is presentation + a slight scaling rule.
Recommended adjustment (minimal rebalance, maximum perceived improvement)
      * Early towns: 10 manuals visible (your current baseline)
      * Cities and higher-tier towns: 14–18 manuals visible
      * Major sect cities / capitals: 20–24 manuals visible
Implementation approach:
      * Keep your 10-slot generation logic as the “core,” but:
      * Add “filler commons” from a deterministic pool to populate shelves without increasing rare output.
      * Or split the 10 generated slots into featured/rare and pad the rest with commons/uncommons.
This preserves rarity economy while making the shop feel stocked.
Refresh cadence
      * Keep your free refresh every 6 hours (good idle re-engagement).
      * Add a “Next refresh in: 02:13:45” timer with clear label.
      * Add a “Refresh history” (last 3 refresh results) to reduce “I missed something” frustration.
NNG explicitly calls out that progress indicators and clear timing reduce confusion and mistrust. (Nielsen Norman Group)
A5.3 Currency design (Tale of Immortal-inspired)
Tale of Immortal uses spirit stones + Mayor’s decree, and reputation gates for special manuals. (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)
You already have equivalents: Gold/Spirit Stones, Merit, etc. Use them in a way players immediately understand.
Currency mapping

      * Spirit Stones: premium purchase currency (rare shelf + featured shelf)

      * Paid refresh tokens
      * Unlocking higher-tier shelves

A5.4 Realm/Grade gating (the “cultivation progression” tie-in)
Your existing grade caps are strong and should remain—they’re one of the few parts that already feel like cultivation progression:

      * Mystic: rank cap 10, trait slots 3, rune sockets 2
Player-facing rule

      * Higher-grade manuals can appear but are “sealed,” with a tooltip:
“Sealed by Heaven’s Law. Breakthrough to Earth Realm to study.”
This is crucial: locked items visible-but-disabled create aspiration and clarity (players understand what they’re missing and why).
A5.5 Pity and fairness (make RNG feel respectful)
Your content bible already includes pity for featured slots: pity10 guarantees Epic, pity30 guarantees Legendary.
The big problem is usually not the existence of pity—it’s that players don’t see it.
UX requirements

         * “Pity: 7/10 → Epic guaranteed”
         * “Legendary pity: 21/30”

This increases trust dramatically.
A5.6 Wishlists + “Ask about Manuals” (agency system)
Tale of Immortal includes NPC interactions like asking about Enigmatic Stones and special quests. (Tale Of Immortal Wiki)

This is how you make the Pavilion feel like a living institution, not a vending machine.

A10. Frontend architecture plan (web UI)
You asked for frontend architect level detail. Here’s the practical architecture that will prevent your UI from collapsing into spaghetti as you add depth.
A10.1 Data model (canonical)
You should treat the system as three entities with clear boundaries:
ManualDefinition (static content)
         * id, name, description, grade, rarity, techniqueId
         * shopTags (path/role/type)
         * icon, lore
TechniqueState (player-owned, persistent)
         * techniqueId
         * grade
         * rarity (or derived from learned manual)
         * rank
         * masteryXP / masteryLevel
         * traits[] (rolled)
         * runeSockets[]
         * equippedLoadouts[] (computed)
PavilionStockState (per-town, refreshable)
         * townId
         * nextRefreshAt
         * slots[]: manualId + price + currencyType + shelfType
         * pityCounters (per featured shelf)
         * focusFilter (optional)
         * wishlistInfluence (optional)
This cleanly supports:
         * visible stock
         * refresh logic
         * buy → manual item → learn → technique state changes
A10.2 Component breakdown (React/Vue-agnostic)
         * <ManualPavilionPage>
         * <PavilionFilters/>
         * <PavilionShelfGrid/>
         * <ManualCard/> (states: locked/affordable/owned duplicate/wishlist)
         * <ManualDetailPanel/>
         * <RefreshBar/> (timer + pity + focus)
         * <ManualSatchelModal/> (learn/store/dismantle)
         * <TechniqueLibraryPage>
         * <LoadoutSelector/>
         * <TechniqueList/> (virtualized list)
         * <TechniqueDetail/>
         * <UpgradeModal/>
         * <TraitRerollModal/>
         * <RuneSocketModal/>
A10.3 State management rules (to avoid bugs)
         * Stock generation and pity counters must live in one place (server or authoritative client store).
         * Buying must be a single transactional action:
         * validate currency
         * remove currency
         * add manual to satchel
         * refresh UI with optimistic update
         * persist
If “manual buys but technique doesn’t unlock,” it’s usually because state updates are split across multiple stores without transaction boundaries.
A10.4 UX microcopy standard (fix “confusing” instantly)
You need consistent phrasing across the entire game:
         * Buy Manual
         * Study Manual
         * Technique Learned
         * Equip Technique
         * Auto-Used in Combat
Never mix:
         * “Unlock/learn/activate/equip” randomly.
Consistency and standards is a core usability heuristic for preventing confusion. (Nielsen Norman Group)

B1. Diagnosis: Why the Current Meditation Hall Fails
Your critique is accurate and the failure is structural, not cosmetic.
B1.1 The mental model is wrong
In cultivation fiction, a Heart Law (心法, xinfa) is the internal method that enables cultivation—your scripture, not a side activity. A wuxia glossary literally defines xinfa as “heart law… the internal training method… used to cultivate qi”. If the game makes this feel like “a separate building you sometimes click,” players will not understand it, and they won’t value it.
B1.2 The UI placement is wrong
Putting Heart Laws behind a “Meditation Hall building” inside every city implies:
                        * it’s city content, not life identity
                        * it’s optional
                        * it’s comparable to apothecary or pavilion (it is not)
Your own architecture already describes Cultivate as a foreground activity (like Outskirts / Trials). It should live where the player expects to start/stop an activity: the Cultivation tab.
B1.3 The system is not “felt”
Even if effects exist, the player currently experiences:
                        * no clear moment of choosing a path
                        * no “scripture identity”
                        * no progress fantasy beat (“I comprehended Verse II”)
                        * no satisfying feedback loop tying cultivation time to power
So it becomes: “pick something, get passive modifiers, forget it.”

B2. Reference Research and Genre Anchors
We’re not copying; we’re copying structures that train the player’s brain.
B2.1 Tale of Immortal: explicit prompting + “new power tool appears”
In Tale of Immortal, after a breakthrough the game explicitly tells the player to check the Manual Pavilion, buy skills, learn them, then equip them—creating a clear causal chain (“I broke through → new system unlocked → I got stronger”).
Your Heart Laws need the same clarity beat—but earlier: at life start.
B2.2 Amazing Cultivation Simulator: “Law” as the cultivation method identity
ACS defines a Law (功法) as a unique cultivation method a character takes, chosen at a key progression point. That’s the same conceptual role as your Heart Law system—except you want it chosen immediately.
B2.3 Dantian/meridians as UI vocabulary
Cultivation fiction borrows heavily from internal alchemy: dantian as an “elixir field” focal point for breath/meditation practice, and meridians as qi “channels”.
We can use this to build intuitive UI metaphors (Qi flow, meridian nodes, breath cycles) that “feel right,” even if the underlying math is idle.

B3. Core Design Goals and Success Criteria
Goals (non-negotiable)
                        1. Immediate identity: within 60 seconds of starting a new life, the player can say:
                        * “I’m a Martial cultivator following Iron Intent Scripture.”
                        2. Zero ambiguity: players understand what Heart Laws do and why they matter.
                        3. Interactive, not annoying: cultivation feels alive when watched, but is set-and-forget when idled.
                        4. Rewarding progression beats: chapters unlock with clear, satisfying power spikes and UI celebrations.
                        5. No “menu tourism”: Heart Law management occurs where you cultivate, not in random buildings.
Success metrics (what we measure)
                        * Selection completion rate: % of new lives that complete Path + Heart Law selection within 90 seconds.
                        * Comprehension engagement: % of users who open Heart Law panel after first chapter unlock.

C0. What’s broken right now (diagnosis)
Your combat “works,” but it fails at the two most basic requirements of an idle auto-combat game:
                                          1. The player cannot read the fight at a glance.
No HP bars, no action pacing cues, no “what just happened” feedback means the game violates a foundational usability principle: visibility of system status (players must see what’s going on and whether their actions had an effect). (Nielsen Norman Group)
                                          2. The player cannot feel progress while watching.
Auto-combat does not have to be “interactive,” but it must be viscerally legible (HP moving, attacks “landing,” skills firing, loot appearing, boss cadence building anticipation). Without those, even good underlying systems feel generic.
                                          3. Outskirts/Trials/Ruins are not differentiated in presentation.
Mechanically they may differ, but the UI isn’t reinforcing the difference. That kills the “place fantasy” (Training Forest vs Spirit Cavern should feel different), and it makes the content feel like a spreadsheet.
                                          4. There’s no “safe to idle” clarity.
In idle combat games, players need a quick answer to: “Can I leave this running?” Melvor’s community frames this as max enemy damage vs your auto-heal/food threshold—a simple, legible safety rule that drives confidence. (Steam Community)
So yes: your combat screen isn’t just “bad looking.” It’s missing required information architecture.

C1. Combat redesign goals and hard constraints
Goals (what the player should feel)
                                             * Immediate readability: In 1 second, the player should know:
                                             * who they’re fighting
                                             * who is winning
                                             * when the next hit/skill lands
                                             * what the last meaningful event was (crit, proc, stun, potion, etc.)
                                             * Idle confidence: A single indicator tells them whether it’s safe to AFK.
                                             * Reward anticipation: Outskirts cadence builds toward boss, boss kills feel like spikes.

Constraints (what we will not do)

We’ll use progressive disclosure: show essentials first, tuck advanced details behind expandable panels. (Nielsen Norman Group)

C7. The Safety Indicator (critical for idle confidence)
This is not optional. This is one of the “missing pillars.”
Melvor players explicitly reason about idling as:

And they surface max damage and special attacks clearly.
Your Safety Badge spec
Badge states:
                                                         * Safe: Expected death chance ~0 in long run (within model)
                                                         * Risky: Can die if specials align / crit chain / low sustain
                                                         * Deadly: Expected death within N fights
Displayed in minibar as 1 word + color + icon
Tooltip explains in plain language:
                                                         * “Enemy max hit: 1,240. Your effective heal per cycle: 1,800. Safe.”
                                                         * “Boss has Poison burst. You lack Cleanse. Risky.”
Implementation details (simple model)
You do not need a perfect simulator. You need a good heuristic:
                                                         * Compute:
                                                         * player EHP = HP / (1 - damageReduction)
                                                         * player sustain = healingPerSecond + shieldPerSecond + lifestealEquivalent
                                                         * enemy DPS = expectedDamagePerSecond (include special attack average)
                                                         * “spike” = enemy max hit / player HP
                                                         * Rules:
                                                         * if spike > 0.9 → Deadly (one-shot risk)
                                                         * else if enemy DPS > sustain → Risky/Deadly depending on margin
                                                         * else Safe
Then refine over time with telemetry.

D1. Apothecary’s purpose in a cultivation idle (design definition)

Part E — Crafting Overhaul: Alchemy Hall + Artifact Forge (Interactive, Rewarding, Still Idle-First)
Right now your Alchemy/Forge experience is “a timer UI that spits out items.” That’s why it feels bland and not cultivation-authentic. In cultivation fantasy, refining is a discipline: flame control, sequencing, timing, stability, impurities, and mastery. The player should feel like they’re actually refining—but because this is an idle game, that interaction must be optional, batch-based, and unlock automation, not become a chore.
This Part E redesign does three things:
                                                                                 1. Keeps your existing queue/offline system intact (because idle-first is non‑negotiable). Your docs explicitly require queue-based professions and “no constant clicking.”
                                                                                 2. Adds a “Hands-on Refining” layer that is fun to watch/do sometimes, and provides meaningful upside (better quality/yield/time + mastery + recipe automation unlock).
                                                                                 3. Makes crafting deeply connected to power loops (techniques, runes, refinement, bounties/expeditions), and makes the UI unmistakably “Alchemy” vs “Forge.”
This design also stays aligned with your core guardrails: single ActivityStore for foreground loops, professions in background, deterministic simulation, and “one click to start, then idle” as the default behavior.

E0 — Non‑Negotiable Design Rules for Crafting in This Game
These are the constraints you already set in your bible/guide, plus the specific implications for interactive crafting:
E0.1 Idle-first defaults (mandatory)
                                                                                 * Default crafting is queue-based and runs while you cultivate/fight/expedition. This is already in your architecture rules and profession prompts.
                                                                                 * Offline progress must advance queues (existing feature).
E0.2 “Hands-on” must be optional and batch-based (not spam)
If hands-on refining is required for routine production, your game stops being idle and becomes a repetitive minigame grinder. That is exactly what your PDF warns against (“zero click-spam”).
So:
                                                                                 * Hands-on sessions are optional.
                                                                                 * Hands-on sessions are short (30–90 seconds).

F.1 Non‑negotiable UX + game design rules for an idle cultivation game
These are the rules we enforce everywhere (including Bounties/Expeditions), because they stop the experience from turning into busywork:
Rule 1: Progressive disclosure or you will drown players
New/advanced features must be hidden until relevant, then introduced with a short guided moment. Progressive disclosure reduces cognitive load and error rate in complex interfaces.
Implementation:
                                                                                                * Locked modules are either:
                                                                                                * Hidden completely (best for early game), or
                                                                                                * Shown as “silhouettes” with one-line unlock requirement (best for mid-game curiosity).
                                                                                                * Never show an interactive panel that cannot be used yet unless it has a clear reason and a clear unlock message.
Rule 2: One foreground activity, everything else is background
If you let players “half-do” five systems at once, the game feels like work. Your own design docs already prescribe the correct structure: one foreground activity (Cultivate / Outskirts / Trial / Ruins / Expeditions / Pagoda), while background queues keep running.
This is exactly how you get “Melvor/NGU depth” without constant attention demands.
Rule 3: Every loop must answer “why does this exist?”

G1. The design premise: idle games are about leaving and returning, not constant interaction
A key property of idle games (as studied in game/HCI research) is that they are built around progress happening while the player is away, and the player returning to make decisions—i.e., a balance between “rules that encourage leaving” and “rules that reward returning.” (ResearchGate)
So your reward cadence must do two things simultaneously:
                                                                                                   1. Make being away feel productive (offline gains, queues, expedition completions).
                                                                                                   2. Make coming back feel like a payoff + choice moment (claim, spend, upgrade, pick next action).
If either side fails, players feel:
                                                                                                   * “Nothing happens” (boring), or
                                                                                                   * “Too many chores” (overwhelming).
________________


G2. Reward schedules: the “cadence grammar” you must use deliberately
A practical, industry-used way to reason about reward cadence is reward schedules: fixed ratio, variable ratio, fixed interval, variable interval. (Game Developer)
G2.1 The four schedules (and what they are good for)
From Game Developer’s breakdown: (Game Developer)
                                                                                                   * Fixed Ratio (FR): reward after doing something a known number of times (e.g., kill 50 mobs).
                                                                                                   * Best for: reliability, clarity, anti-frustration.
                                                                                                   * Variable Ratio (VR): reward after a random/semi-random number of actions (drops).
                                                                                                   * Best for: excitement, “maybe next one” tension.
                                                                                                   * Fixed Interval (FI): reward once per set time interval (daily claim, shop refresh).
                                                                                                   * Best for: re-engagement clocks and planned play.
                                                                                                   * Variable Interval (VI): reward after random time intervals (rarely good alone).
                                                                                                   * Best for: layered on top of something else, carefully, because it can confuse players. (Game Developer)
G2.2 The core rule for your game
Main progression must be FR/FI (deterministic).
Excitement can be VR—BUT must be bonus-only and have pity/conversion.
The same article explicitly warns that variable ratio feels better when it’s “a bonus on top of core gameplay” rather than a requirement for continued play. (Game Developer)
This is directly relevant to your current “techniques feel bla bla bla” and “expeditions don’t feel rewarding” complaints: too much of your progress is currently felt as VR noise with no structured FR/FI payoff ladder.

G3. The economy blueprint: faucets, sinks, converters, and inflation control
If your currencies are not intentionally structured, you get the two classic failure modes:
                                                                                                   * Dead currency (players stockpile because nothing feels worth buying), or
                                                                                                   * Inflation (currency becomes meaningless because faucets outpace sinks).
G3.1 Define the economy pieces
A “sink” (aka drain) removes currency/resources from the economy and helps prevent inflation, but must be balanced against sources. (Game Developer)
Machinations describes inflation as rising money supply reducing currency value; if mishandled, mechanics cease to work because currency loses meaning. (Machinations)
G3.2 Your economy should have three layers (not one)
This is the simplest structure that stays deep without becoming messy:
                                                                                                   1. Flow Currency (Soft): Gold / Spirit Stones
                                                                                                   * Earned constantly. Spent constantly.
                                                                                                   2. Direction Currency (Meta Utility): Merit / City Decree
                                                                                                   * Earned via bounties. Spent on targeted acceleration (refreshes, scrolls, pity).
                                                                                                   3. Power Materials: Fragments, Ink, Dust, Rare Herbs/Ores
                                                                                                   * Earned via specific loops. Spent on upgrades in those loops.
Why three layers: it stops “one currency solves everything” and it lets you tune frustration relief without breaking progression.

G4. Your “Cadence Grid”: the exact time-scales your game must satisfy
If you don’t explicitly design reward cadence at multiple time scales, the game will always feel flat.
Here’s the target grid for a web idle cultivation game:
0–10 seconds: micro-feedback (always-on)
Purpose: make the simulation feel alive.
Examples:
                                                                                                   * HP bars moving, damage numbers, “loot ticker” increments, small mastery ticks.
This is directly supported by UX research: users need visibility of system status to know what’s happening and what to do next. (Nielsen Norman Group)
30–120 seconds: micro-goals
Purpose: give quick “I accomplished something” hits.
Examples:
                                                                                                   * kill streak reward (FR),
                                                                                                   * “first boss spawn” progress (FR),
                                                                                                   * first small loot highlight (VR bonus).
5–15 minutes: session reward beats
Purpose: create “I should claim and spend” moments.
Examples:
                                                                                                   * bounty completion (FR),
                                                                                                   * expedition completion (FI),
                                                                                                   * ruins run completion (FR),
                                                                                                   * a new manual choice appears (FI refresh).
1–4 hours: medium milestones
Purpose: create meaningful upgrades without requiring long grinds.
Examples:
                                                                                                   * manual rarity tier upgrade,
                                                                                                   * technique mastery threshold,
                                                                                                   * recipe mastery threshold unlocking better automation,
                                                                                                   * a cultivation verse completion / breakthrough attempt.
1 day: daily anchor
Purpose: predictable re-engagement.
Examples:
                                                                                                   * 1–2 meaningful shop refreshes (FI),
                                                                                                   * a guaranteed bounty set reset (FI),
                                                                                                   * “daily decree” claim (FI).
1–2 weeks: long arc goals
Purpose: retention without chores.
Examples:
                                                                                                   * city unlock,
                                                                                                   * new heart law path,
                                                                                                   * prestige milestone.

G5. System-by-system reward schedule mapping (what each system should be)
This is where your current game is failing: the schedules are not intentional enough.
G5.1 Outskirts (auto-combat)
Primary schedule: Fixed Ratio (FR) + visible progress
                                                                                                   * “Boss in X kills” bar (FR)
                                                                                                   * “Elite in Y minutes” meter (FI/FR hybrid)
Excitement schedule: Variable Ratio (VR) drops, but bonus-only
                                                                                                   * rare manual fragment drop, rare herb drop, etc.
Anti-drought: pity/conversion
                                                                                                   * If a “rare” doesn’t drop, you still earn “Beast Core Shards” (FR) that can be exchanged for the rare after N runs.
Why: VR drives excitement, but FR prevents frustration and makes progress feel earned. (Game Developer)
G5.2 Gate Trials (milestone checks)
Primary schedule: Fixed Ratio (FR)
                                                                                                   * “Clear Trial floor” = guaranteed meaningful reward.
Trials should be where Spirit Stones and/or Merit show up reliably, because this is where players prove power, not roll dice.
G5.3 Ruins (resource loop)
Primary schedule: FR (rooms cleared) + FI (run completion)
                                                                                                   * predictable mats per room,
                                                                                                   * run completion chest.
Bonus: VR on “artifact shard” with pity.
G5.4 Bounties (direction loop)
Primary schedule: FR objectives + FI board refresh
This is exactly why Tale of Immortal’s bounty board feels good: it’s structured goals that pay out predictable currencies that matter for town systems.
In Tale of Immortal, the quest board awards spirit stones and “Mayor Decrees,” which are used for the Manual Pavilion. (Steam Community)
That’s the model you want: bounties are not “busywork”; they are the targeted accelerator for the town’s most important rewards.
G5.5 Expeditions (reliable resources)
Primary schedule: FI completion
                                                                                                   * you choose a duration, get a predictable yield band.
Bonus: VR rare drop per expedition, but with a pity counter.
This aligns with idle game design fundamentals: progress happens while away, and returning gives you options. (ResearchGate)
G5.6 Manual Pavilion (core excitement + power choice)
Primary schedule: FI refresh + FR “pity fragments”
                                                                                                   * pavilion stock refresh is a clock,
                                                                                                   * duplicate manuals always convert into fragments (FR deterministic progress),
                                                                                                   * wishlist targeting uses a currency sink (Merit/Decree).
In Tale of Immortal, Manual Pavilion access is gated by Spirit Stones plus a secondary currency (Mayor’s decree), and some manuals require reputation. (tale-of-immortal.fandom.com)
You don’t need reputation right now, but the key principle is: manual acquisition is structured, not random-only.
G5.7 Professions (alchemy/forge)
Primary schedule: FI craft completion (queue)
Skill payoff: hands-on crafting grants “quality bonus” (VR-ish outcomes) but also deterministic mastery progress (FR).
This is how you keep interactivity fun without making it labor.

	G6.2 Faucet/sink balancing: the hard rule
You must balance sinks to remove enough currency relative to sources, or inflation makes currency meaningless. (Game Developer)
Practical tuning heuristic:
Every major currency must have:
                                                                                                   * at least one always-available sink (so it never becomes dead), and
                                                                                                   * at least one aspirational sink (so players care about stockpiling a bit).
If a currency has only aspirational sinks, early/mid players hoard and feel stuck.
If it has only always-available sinks, late players feel nothing is exciting.
G6.3 “Good-feeling” sinks vs “bad-feeling” sinks
                                                                                                   * Good-feeling sinks: players spend and feel smarter/stronger.
Examples: reroll with guardrails, targeting, upgrades that are visible in combat.
                                                                                                   * Bad-feeling sinks: players spend and feel punished.
Examples: taxes, mandatory fees, random loss.
If you need inflation control, do it with thematic converters (spend gold → get something useful) rather than pure deletion, because your game is single-player and should feel empowering.
________________


G7. Tuning targets: the “time-to-upgrade budgets” that prevent boredom
You cannot tune by “numbers feel good.” You tune by time-to-meaningful-upgrade (TTMU).
G7.1 Define your upgrade tiers
Use a consistent ladder:
                                                                                                      * Minor upgrade: noticeable within a minute.
                                                                                                      * Target TTMU: 1–3 minutes
                                                                                                      * Medium upgrade: changes build decisions.
                                                                                                      * Target TTMU: 10–25 minutes
                                                                                                      * Major upgrade: new manual tier / new mechanic threshold.
                                                                                                      * Target TTMU: 1–3 hours
                                                                                                      * Arc milestone: city unlock / prestige threshold.
                                                                                                      * Target TTMU: 1–7 days (depending on game length goals)
G7.2 The math you actually use
Use the idle balancing approach advocated by the “Math of Idle Games” series: treat your systems as generators/exchange rates and reason about cost vs production. (Game Developer)
At minimum, you need this formula in your tuning sheet:
TTMU = Cost / NetIncomeRate
Where:
                                                                                                      * NetIncomeRate = (income faucets) − (mandatory maintenance sinks) per time unit.
This is how you guarantee pacing.
G7.3 Prestige / Ascension pacing (keep numbers manageable and exciting)
Prestige loops exist because they create a “ladder climbing” effect (reset → big multiplier → new climb) and they rein numbers back into a manageable range. (Kongregate Newsroom)
So for your cultivation-themed prestige:
                                                                                                      * Ascension should not be “end of content.”
                                                                                                      * It should be a new cycle with:
                                                                                                      * new pavilion tiers,
                                                                                                      * new heart law variants,
                                                                                                      * new “Dao Marks” (meta nodes),
                                                                                                      * and stronger long-term automation.
________________


G8. Anti-frustration engineering: RNG excitement without RNG dependency
This is where most idle games fail.
G8.1 The correct layering pattern
Layer reward schedules: e.g., fixed interval rewards with variable ratio variability on top. The reward schedules article gives the Warframe example: daily login (FI) with variable rewards layered (VR). (Game Developer)
For your game:
                                                                                                      * Every important VR drop must have a deterministic shadow:
                                                                                                      * fragments,
                                                                                                      * pity counters,
                                                                                                      * targeted currency.
G8.2 Practical pity rules (use the same everywhere)
Set a universal pity approach:
                                                                                                      * Every “rare” outcome has:
                                                                                                      * baseChance
                                                                                                      * pityIncrement per failure
                                                                                                      * pityCap (guarantee at N tries)
And every “duplicate” outcome converts to a deterministic currency.
This prevents the “techniques feel unrewarding” problem because even “bad luck” is still progress.

G9. Reward presentation: your UI must sell the reward or it doesn’t exist
You can have perfect numbers and still feel unrewarding if feedback is weak.
G9.1 Visibility of system status is non-negotiable
If users can’t tell what’s happening, they can’t decide what to do next. (Nielsen Norman Group)
In your game, that means:
                                                                                                      * Every loop needs a visible:
                                                                                                      * progress bar,
                                                                                                      * next milestone,
                                                                                                      * and “why this matters” line.
G9.2 Progressive disclosure to prevent UI overwhelm
You must show only the most important info first, and reveal advanced detail on demand. (Nielsen Norman Group)
Applied to rewards:
                                                                                                      * Default reward summary shows:
                                                                                                      * 3–5 headline items (biggest impact)
                                                                                                      * Expand reveals:
                                                                                                      * full itemization (mats, fragments, byproducts, etc.)
G9.3 “Reward ceremonies” (implementation-ready patterns)
You need three standardized patterns:
                                                                                                      1. Micro Toast (0–2 seconds)
                                                                                                      * “+12 Gold, +1 Fragment”
                                                                                                      2. Chest Summary (boss/trial/ruins)
                                                                                                      * Spotlight top reward
                                                                                                      * “Best Drop” callout
                                                                                                      * “Suggested Next Action” buttons (Equip / Upgrade / Spend Merit)
                                                                                                      3. Milestone Modal (manual tier, verse completion, mastery threshold)
                                                                                                      * one clear message
                                                                                                      * one clear next choice
If you don’t do this, the game will stay “numbers happen in the background” and remain emotionally flat.

G10. Economy tuning workflow (how you iterate without chaos)
G10.1 Build the “Economy Ledger” spreadsheet (mandatory)
Your ledger needs:
                                                                                                      * Rows: every activity (Outskirts tier, Trial tier, Ruins tier, Expedition duration, Bounty difficulty)
                                                                                                      * Columns: time, gold, stones, merit, fragments, mats, expected value, variance, pity step
Then compute:
                                                                                                      * TTMU for each key purchase (manual cost, refresh cost, recipe unlock)
                                                                                                      * % of income spent on sinks (target 60–85% for flow currencies in midgame; lower early, higher late)
This is exactly the kind of balancing work the idle-math literature recommends: use models/spreadsheets to reason about progression without manually playing every permutation. (Game Developer)
G10.2 Put all rates behind remote config
Even if you’re not “live ops,” you still want:
                                                                                                      * a single config file (or remote JSON) controlling:
                                                                                                      * drop rates,
                                                                                                      * payout bands,
                                                                                                      * sink costs,
                                                                                                      * pity parameters.
G10.3 What to test first (highest ROI)
                                                                                                      1. Early game cadence: does the player get a meaningful manual and feel power increase fast?
                                                                                                      2. RNG drought: can a player progress with bad luck?
                                                                                                      3. Currency deadness: do players stockpile gold/merit/stones with nothing worth buying?

H5 — State management standards (Zustand done correctly)
Zustand is unopinionated, which is both power and danger. You must adopt discipline or you’ll recreate the “confusing and generic” problem at the code level.
H5.1 Store design rules (non-negotiable)
                                                                                                      1. Stores own state + actions only. No JSX, no DOM, no timers created inside React components.
                                                                                                      2. No “god store.” But also don’t make 50 tiny stores that can’t coordinate.
                                                                                                      3. Cross-store communication happens via:
                                                                                                      * explicit service calls (RewardService),
                                                                                                      * or typed events (CombatResolved),
                                                                                                      * not random direct getState() chains everywhere.
H5.2 Selector discipline (performance and correctness)
If you do this:
const store = useCombatStore();


you re-render the component on every combat store change.
Instead, always use selectors:
                                                                                                      * Zustand’s useStore hook is explicitly designed to return selected state via a selector function. (zustand.docs.pmnd.rs)
                                                                                                      * For subscriptions and side effects, use subscribeWithSelector middleware (so you only react to what matters). (GitHub)
Required pattern
                                                                                                      * Every store exports:
                                                                                                      * useXStore(selector)
                                                                                                      * selectX helpers in stores/selectors/ for common derived reads
                                                                                                      * actions grouped and named consistently
H5.3 React concurrency safety (external store sync)
React’s useSyncExternalStore is the “correct” way to subscribe to external stores in concurrent rendering. Libraries like Zustand integrate this pattern. (React)
Practical takeaway:
                                                                                                      * Don’t build custom subscription hacks.
                                                                                                      * Don’t run “tick loops” in React component state.

H7 — Time, ticks, and offline catch-up (idle game backbone)
This is where most web idle games get janky: timers drift, saves corrupt, and offline progress double-applies.
H7.1 The GameClock (single source of time)
Implement a GameClock service that provides:
                                                                                                         * nowWall() = Date.now() (for saves and “time away”)
                                                                                                         * nowMono() = performance.now() (for in-session frame deltas; monotonic and not affected by system clock skew) (MDN Web Docs)
Why:
                                                                                                         * Date.now() can jump if the system clock changes.
                                                                                                         * performance.now() is stable and high resolution for smooth in-session ticking. (MDN Web Docs)
H7.2 Tick rates (don’t tick everything at 60fps)
Idle games are mostly numbers. Your goal is “smooth enough,” not “real-time action.”
Recommended:
                                                                                                         * Combat simulation tick: 5–10 Hz (every 100–200ms) with interpolation for UI bars
                                                                                                         * Cultivation tick: 1 Hz (or “accumulate by delta” every N seconds)
                                                                                                         * Queues: event-driven (next completion timestamp), not per-frame polling
This reduces CPU, reduces React re-renders, and makes offline math easier.
H7.3 Offline catch-up strategy (apply only what should run offline)
Your plan says offline applies to cultivation + profession queues + expeditions, and explicitly not combat.
Implement OfflineCatchup.apply(saveState, nowWall):
                                                                                                         * Compute delta seconds since last save
                                                                                                         * Apply delta to:
                                                                                                         * cultivation (if meditating, or whatever your design says)
                                                                                                         * profession queues
                                                                                                         * expeditions
                                                                                                         * Do not simulate combat loops offline
                                                                                                         * Update lastActive timestamp to prevent double application
UI requirement
After catch-up, show a banner:
                                                                                                         * “+X Qi from Yh offline” and “+Z items finished in Alchemy”
This is critical “visibility of system status.” (Nielsen Norman Group)
H7.4 Avoid freezing the UI (chunk offline work)
If catch-up could be heavy (long queues, many jobs), process in chunks using idle time APIs:
                                                                                                            * requestIdleCallback schedules low-priority work without harming responsiveness. (MDN Web Docs)

Part I — Validation, Telemetry, and Proof the Game Is No Longer Confusing
How we measure clarity, reward satisfaction, and progression health; how we instrument; how we iterate without guessing.
This part is the “evidence plan.” Without it, you’ll keep shipping “more features” and still get the same feedback: confusing, generic, unrewarding—because you won’t have a repeatable way to prove the experience improved.
I’m going to give you:
                                                                                                                  * A metrics framework (what “better” means)
                                                                                                                  * A telemetry plan (what to track, naming, schema, where to hook it)
                                                                                                                  * A qualitative research plan (usability tests that catch confusion fast)
                                                                                                                  * A validation gate (release criteria + regression checks)
                                                                                                                  * A playtest + experiment menu (what to A/B and why)
This is grounded in established UX measurement frameworks (Google HEART), usability evaluation methods (Nielsen heuristics + cognitive walkthroughs), and online experimentation best practices (Kohavi). (Google Research)

I1. The validation framework: HEART + OEC
You need one framework for “UX quality,” plus one metric that acts as your “ship gate.”
I1.1 HEART (Google) for UX quality
Google’s HEART framework is explicitly designed to measure UX at scale using five categories: Happiness, Engagement, Adoption, Retention, Task Success. (Google Research)
For your game, HEART prevents a classic mistake: “Retention went up, so UX must be good.” Not true. A grindy loop can inflate retention while still being confusing. HEART forces you to measure both behavior and usability.
I1.2 OEC (Overall Evaluation Criterion) for “ship/no-ship”
Kohavi’s controlled experimentation work pushes an important practice: define an Overall Evaluation Criterion so you don’t optimize for shallow metrics (like clicks) that harm long-term outcomes. (ai.stanford.edu)
Your OEC should be a small weighted set, not 30 metrics. Example below.
________________


I2. What “no longer confusing” means (explicit goals and falsifiable claims)
You do not validate with “feels better.” You validate by writing claims you can disprove.
I2.1 The top 8 claims we must validate (mapped to your critiques)
                                                                                                                  1. Manual Pavilion is understandable and rewarding
                                                                                                                  * Players can buy → learn → equip → see impact without guidance.
                                                                                                                  2. Heart Law selection is inseparable from Cultivation
                                                                                                                  * Players choose path + heart law at life start; can re-open from Cultivation tab; they understand it drives cultivation.
                                                                                                                  3. Combat is watchable and readable
                                                                                                                  * Players can tell who is winning, why they died, and whether it’s safe to idle (status visibility). (Nielsen Norman Group)
                                                                                                                  4. Apothecary feels distinct and its purpose is obvious
                                                                                                                  * Players can prep for combat/cultivation in <30 seconds.
                                                                                                                  5. Alchemy/Forge are fun once, efficient forever
                                                                                                                  * Players try a hands-on session and later automate (optional interactivity, not chores).
                                                                                                                  6. Bounties give direction, not busywork
                                                                                                                  * Players understand what bounties are for and complete at least one naturally during normal play.
                                                                                                                  7. Expeditions feel rewarding and visually satisfying
                                                                                                                  * Claim moments feel like payoff; results route into the next action.
                                                                                                                  8. Global UI supports progressive disclosure (not overwhelm)
                                                                                                                  * Early players see fewer systems; they unlock with contextual teaching. Progressive disclosure makes complex apps easier to learn and less error-prone. (Nielsen Norman Group)

I3. Metrics: a concrete HEART scorecard for your game
This is the core deliverable: Goals → Signals → Metrics per HEART category (Google’s recommended structure). (Google Research)
I3.1 Happiness (Do players like the experience?)
Goal: “The game feels clear and rewarding.”
Signals:
                                                                                                                  * Perceived ease of use
                                                                                                                  * Perceived reward satisfaction
Metrics:
                                                                                                                  * SUS score after first session and after Day 3
                                                                                                                  * SUS is widely used as a quick, low-cost usability measure. (digital.ahrq.gov)
                                                                                                                  * Interpretation reference points:
                                                                                                                  * SUS ~68 is often treated as “average,” while ~80 is closer to “excellent.” (Bentley University)
                                                                                                                  * “Confusion score” micro-survey (3 questions, 1–5 scale):
                                                                                                                  * “I know what to do next.”
                                                                                                                  * “I understand what manuals do.”
                                                                                                                  * “Combat outcomes feel understandable.”
Hard gate suggestion: SUS ≥ 75 for your target audience build (not “average”), because “average usability” is usually not competitive. (Bentley University)
I3.2 Engagement (Are they actively using the systems?)
Goal: “Players interact with the intended core loops (without chores).”
Metrics:
                                                                                                                  * Active session length (median)
                                                                                                                  * Foreground activity distribution (Cultivation vs Outskirts vs Ruins vs Expeditions)
                                                                                                                  * Combat Theater opens per session (watchability proxy)
                                                                                                                  * Manual equip changes per session (build engagement proxy)
I3.3 Adoption (Are they using new/important features?)
Goal: “Players successfully discover and use the key pillars.”
Adoption milestones (binary per user):
                                                                                                                  * Picked a heart law
                                                                                                                  * Bought a manual
                                                                                                                  * Equipped a manual
                                                                                                                  * Completed a bounty
                                                                                                                  * Claimed an expedition
                                                                                                                  * Crafted a pill or weapon once (hands-on optional)
                                                                                                                  * Used apothecary pouch / consumable slot
I3.4 Retention (Do they come back?)
Goal: “Players return after understanding the game.”
Metrics:
                                                                                                                  * D1 / D7 retention (standard definition)
                                                                                                                  * GameAnalytics describes retention as the percent of users who installed and returned N days later. (GameAnalytics Documentation)
                                                                                                                  * Rolling retention (optional secondary)
                                                                                                                  * Return after first “big moment” (first manual equip, first breakthrough, first boss chest)
Important note: D1 places a hard cap on later retention (if D1 collapses, D7/D30 cannot be good). (Solsten)
I3.5 Task Success (Can they do key tasks without confusion?)
Goal: “Players can complete core tasks quickly and correctly.”
Metrics:
                                                                                                                  * Task completion rate (pass/fail)
                                                                                                                  * Time on task
                                                                                                                  * Error rate (wrong screen, backtracking, repeated clicks, dead ends)
This is where usability testing comes in; see I7.

I4. Your OEC (one metric to rule release decisions)
If you do A/B tests or even just iterative releases, you need a single “scoreboard” metric to prevent optimizing the wrong thing.
I4.1 Recommended OEC (practical, not academic)
OEC = 0.35 * Onboarding Task Success + 0.25 * D1 Retention + 0.20 * SUS + 0.20 * “Meaningful Progress per Hour”
Where:
                                                                                                                  * Onboarding Task Success = % of new users who complete the core onboarding funnel (defined below) within their first session.
                                                                                                                  * Meaningful Progress per Hour = count of “meaningful upgrades” earned per active hour (manual purchase/equip, recipe mastery milestone, trial clear, breakthrough attempt, etc.)
Why this structure:
                                                                                                                  * It prevents “click optimization” traps—Kohavi warns explicitly to optimize for the right long-term criterion, not shallow clickthrough. (ai.stanford.edu)

Goal: prove the player understands the game.
Steps:

I6. Telemetry design: events, naming conventions, schemas, and versioning
Telemetry is only useful if you record the right data in a consistent way. Game Developer’s telemetry-supported design article stresses you must identify what data needs recording (sessions, retention, and game-specific data). (Game Developer)
I6.1 Tracking plan is mandatory (no “random track() calls”)
Use a tracking plan that lives in the repo. Segment’s best practices call for:
                                                                                                                  * Consistent casing
                                                                                                                  * Consistent event name structure (Object + Action)
                                                                                                                  * Avoiding inconsistent definitions (Twilio)
Snowplow similarly emphasizes consistent naming and reusable entities to make analysis easier. (Snowplow Documentation)
PostHog explicitly recommends:
                                                                                                                  * implement naming conventions
                                                                                                                  * version your events
                                                                                                                  * filter out internal users (PostHog)
I6.2 Event naming convention (recommended)
                                                                                                                  * Events: Title Case and Object + Action
Examples:
                                                                                                                     * Manual Purchased
                                                                                                                     * Manual Equipped
                                                                                                                     * Heart Law Selected
                                                                                                                     * Outskirts Started
                                                                                                                     * Properties: snake_case
Examples:
                                                                                                                        * manual_id, city_id, realm_tier, source_context
This matches Segment’s recommended approach. (Twilio)
I6.3 Event versioning (to avoid breaking dashboards)
Every event must include:
                                                                                                                        * event_version: 1 (increment if meaning changes)
                                                                                                                        * schema_version: "2025-12-23" (or semantic version)
Versioning is a best practice because event meaning drifts over time; PostHog calls this out directly. (PostHog)
I6.4 Global entities captured with every event
Capture consistent context fields (Snowplow-style “entities” concept): (Snowplow Documentation)
                                                                                                                        * user_id (anonymous ID, not PII)
                                                                                                                        * session_id
                                                                                                                        * life_id (important for your game)
                                                                                                                        * city_id
                                                                                                                        * realm and realm_stage
                                                                                                                        * active_foreground_activity
                                                                                                                        * client_build_version
                                                                                                                        * content_pack_version
This makes analysis dramatically easier and prevents “we can’t segment by life” problems.

I9. Qualitative validation (how we actually catch “confusing”)
Numbers tell you what is happening. Usability methods tell you why.
I9.1 Heuristic evaluation (fast internal review)
Use Nielsen’s 10 usability heuristics as a structured checklist. The #1 heuristic—visibility of system status—is directly relevant to your combat and progression clarity problems. (Nielsen Norman Group)
Process:
                                                                                                                           * 2–4 reviewers (designer + dev + “not familiar” teammate)
                                                                                                                           * Review top flows (below)
                                                                                                                           * Log issues as: heuristic violated → screenshot → severity → suggested fix
I9.2 Cognitive walkthrough (learnability inspection)
NNG describes cognitive walkthroughs as a task-based method where reviewers step through a flow and answer prescribed questions to identify learnability issues. (Nielsen Norman Group)
Use this for:
                                                                                                                           * First-life onboarding
                                                                                                                           * Manual buy/equip
                                                                                                                           * Heart law selection
                                                                                                                           * Bounty claim and “go do this”
                                                                                                                           * Expedition claim → use materials
Walkthrough questions (simple, effective):
                                                                                                                           1. Will the user know what to do at this step?
                                                                                                                           2. Will they see the control/action they need?
                                                                                                                           3. Will they understand the feedback and know it worked?
                                                                                                                           4. If they make a mistake, can they recover?
I9.3 Moderated usability tests (real people, real confusion)
Format:
                                                                                                                           * 5–8 participants per iteration (you’ll catch most severe issues quickly)
                                                                                                                           * 30–45 minutes each
                                                                                                                           * Think-aloud protocol (they verbalize reasoning)
Tasks (pass/fail + timing):
                                                                                                                           1. “Pick a path and heart law, start cultivating.”
                                                                                                                           2. “Go fight in the outskirts and tell me if you’re winning.”
                                                                                                                           3. “Buy a manual and equip it so it’s used in combat.”
                                                                                                                           4. “Complete and claim a bounty; spend Merit on something useful.”
                                                                                                                           5. “Start an expedition; claim it; use the materials.”
Post-test questionnaire: SUS
SUS is designed to be a quick, reliable global usability assessment. (digital.ahrq.gov)
Why SUS matters here:
It gives you a single comparative number between builds. If your SUS does not improve after UI clarity work, you didn’t fix the real problem.

I10. Experimentation plan (A/B tests you should actually run)
Controlled experiments let you establish causality (feature change → behavior change). Kohavi’s guide is the practical bible: define a clear evaluation criterion (OEC), avoid HiPPO decision-making, and optimize for long-term outcomes. (ai.stanford.edu)
I10.1 The experiment menu (high-impact, low-risk)
                                                                                                                           1. Manual Pavilion onboarding prompt

Part J — Character/Status, Equipment, and Build Readability Overhaul
Internal reference baseline: your current global nav includes Character/Status as a top-level tab (Part F), your combat UI needs visible DPS/EHP/TTK signals (Part C), and your crafting/forge systems generate/upgrade gear-adjacent outputs (Part E). This part is the missing “player sheet” that makes all those systems feel coherent instead of like disconnected panels.
J0. What “good” looks like for this system
If you build nothing else beyond what’s already in Parts A–I, but you build this Character/Status overhaul correctly, your game immediately feels 2–3x more “real” because players can finally answer:
Within 60 seconds (new player):
                                                                                                                              * “What am I wearing?”
                                                                                                                              * “Why is my damage low?”
                                                                                                                              * “What should I upgrade next?”
                                                                                                                              * “If I equip this, what changes?”
Within 2 minutes (experienced idle player):
                                                                                                                              * “What’s my DPS/EHP/TTK and what is driving it?”
                                                                                                                              * “Which technique(s) are carrying the run?”
                                                                                                                              * “Where are my multipliers coming from (Heart Law vs gear vs buffs)?”
Non‑negotiable success criteria
                                                                                                                              1. Instant comparison: any loot/equipment change shows a clear delta in DPS, EHP, and at least one build-relevant tag (e.g., “Burn build +12% synergy”).
                                                                                                                              2. Recognition over recall: players never have to remember where a stat comes from; they can drill down from summary → breakdown → exact sources. (This is directly aligned to recognition-over-recall UX guidance.) (Nielsen Norman Group)
                                                                                                                              3. Progressive disclosure: novices see simple “Power” + “DPS/EHP” + “Next upgrade”, while experts can expand formulas and per-source modifiers. (Nielsen Norman Group)
                                                                                                                              4. No spreadsheet trap: you can expose formulas, but only behind an “Advanced” disclosure and always with plain-English explanations.
J1. Diagnosis: why Character/Status is currently a silent failure
Right now, your doc is excellent on systems, but the player lacks a single “truth screen” where those systems resolve into a build identity.
Typical player failure modes in your current structure:
                                                                                                                              * They can’t tell if gear matters (because combat is auto and effects are not legible enough).
                                                                                                                              * They can’t tell what to keep (inventory becomes junk pile).
                                                                                                                              * They can’t tell what drives their performance (Heart Laws, techniques, and consumables all contribute but don’t reconcile).
This is why idle games often feel “generic dashboard” when they lack a strong character sheet: the player has no anchor for the meta loop.
J2. Core design rules (do not compromise)
J2.1 One screen = one job
Character/Status is not a second Inventory. It has one job: explain the current build and the next best upgrade path.
J2.2 The sheet must compute the same numbers the combat sim uses
No “approximate power score” that diverges from real combat math. If you do include a “Power” score, it must be derived from the same DPS/EHP/TTK calculations you already plan to show in Part C.
J2.3 Everything is traceable
Every stat line supports:
                                                                                                                              * “What is this?” tooltip
                                                                                                                              * “Where did it come from?” breakdown (sources list)
                                                                                                                              * “What is the next upgrade that affects it?” hint (optional)
This is a direct application of visibility-of-system-status and recognition-over-recall principles. (Nielsen Norman Group)
J3. Character/Status tab: Information Architecture and Layout
Top-level layout (desktop): 3 columns, scroll-stable.
Left column — Identity & Loadouts
                                                                                                                              * Character portrait + realm/substage
                                                                                                                              * Current Spirit Root (and resonance tags)
                                                                                                                              * Active Heart Law + signature effect summary
                                                                                                                              * Loadout selector (Build A / B / C)
                                                                                                                              * “Switch Loadout” (disabled if in combat)
                                                                                                                              * “Share build” (creates a compact export string)
Center column — Equipment Paper Doll + Quick Compare
                                                                                                                              * Equipment slots grid (weapon, armor, accessory, talisman, relic/artifact, boots, ring, amulet—theme names are flexible)
                                                                                                                              * Clicking a slot opens: “All items for this slot” (Inventory filtered view)
                                                                                                                              * “Recommended upgrade” indicator on slots (small badge, not mandatory)
                                                                                                                              * “Auto-equip suggestions” toggle (off by default)
Right column — Performance Summary
                                                                                                                              * DPS, EHP, TTK vs current enemy tier
                                                                                                                              * “Damage breakdown” (expandable):
                                                                                                                              * Base attack
                                                                                                                              * Technique contribution
                                                                                                                              * DoT contribution
                                                                                                                              * Crit multiplier
                                                                                                                              * Elemental multipliers
                                                                                                                              * “Survivability breakdown” (expandable):
                                                                                                                              * HP
                                                                                                                              * Damage reduction / armor
                                                                                                                              * Shields
                                                                                                                              * Evasion
                                                                                                                              * Healing per second / sustain
                                                                                                                              * “Next best upgrades” (3 items, always actionable links):
                                                                                                                              * “Forge: refine weapon to +2 (cost: …)”
                                                                                                                              * “Manual Pavilion: upgrade Technique X to Rank 4 (cost: …)”
                                                                                                                              * “Alchemy: craft Healing Pellet recipe (needs …)”
This is where the game stops feeling generic. It turns your systems into a readable build story.
J4. Equipment system definition (slots, item types, and constraints)
J4.1 Slots (recommendation)
Pick 8 slots to start. More slots means more clutter and weaker per-slot meaning.
Suggested slots (cultivation-appropriate, simple):
                                                                                                                              1. Weapon (Sword/Spear/Blade/Talisman focus)
                                                                                                                              2. Robe (armor)
                                                                                                                              3. Boots
                                                                                                                              4. Ring
                                                                                                                              5. Amulet
                                                                                                                              6. Talisman (defensive charm)
                                                                                                                              7. Relic/Artifact (rare slot; unlocks later)
                                                                                                                              8. Belt/Token (optional; if you want exactly 8)
J4.2 Early-game gating
                                                                                                                              * Start with Weapon + Robe + Ring unlocked
                                                                                                                              * Unlock 1 slot per city tier milestone (ties to Part F city progression)
                                                                                                                              * Locked slots visible with “Unlock at: Foundation Establishment” style text
This uses aspiration without confusion (players see what’s coming).
J4.3 Item identity: “Base + Affixes + (optional) Unique Effect”
                                                                                                                                 * Base stats = predictable scaling by realm tier
                                                                                                                                 * Affixes = build hooks (element, technique family, sustain, etc.)
                                                                                                                                 * Unique = rare chase item that changes play pattern (optional; do not overuse)
J5. Stat model (simple core, deep drill-down)
Your game can support deep stats, but the player-facing model must be legible.
J5.1 Core attributes (keep to 4)
                                                                                                                                 * Body: HP, physical resist, regen scaling
                                                                                                                                 * Spirit: Qi max, technique potency scaling, elemental power
                                                                                                                                 * Agility: attack speed, evade, crit rate
                                                                                                                                 * Will: status resistance, shield strength, sustain modifiers
J5.2 Derived combat metrics (always visible in summary)
                                                                                                                                 * DPS: expected damage per second vs current enemy archetype
                                                                                                                                 * EHP: effective HP vs current enemy archetype
                                                                                                                                 * TTK: time to kill current enemy baseline (or current boss)
These are the same “what matters” metrics you already plan to surface in combat UI. (Part C already calls out DPS/EHP/TTK visibility.)
J5.3 Breakdown rules
                                                                                                                                    * Every derived metric must list contributing sources:
                                                                                                                                    * Base stats
                                                                                                                                    * Gear stats
                                                                                                                                    * Heart Law mods
                                                                                                                                    * Technique passive mods
                                                                                                                                    * Consumable buffs
                                                                                                                                    * Each source line shows: +X (from ItemName / HeartLaw / Technique)
This supports recognition-over-recall. (Nielsen Norman Group)
J5.4 Advanced formula disclosure
Under “Advanced”, show formulas in human readable form, not raw algebra. Example:
EHP (vs Physical)
                                                                                                                                       * Damage Reduction = 1 − (DamageTakenMultiplier)
                                                                                                                                       * DamageTakenMultiplier sources:
                                                                                                                                       * Armor: −18%
                                                                                                                                       * Talisman ward: −6%
                                                                                                                                       * Heart Law (Stone Skin): −10%
                                                                                                                                       * Enemy pierce: +5%
Net multiplier: 0.71 → EHP = HP / 0.71
This is progressive disclosure in practice: simple first, deep later. (Nielsen Norman Group)
J6. Itemization: rarity, affix pools, and build hooks
J6.1 Rarity tiers (recommendation)
                                                                                                                                          * Common → Uncommon → Rare → Epic → Legendary
(If you add Mythic, only do it post-prestige.)
Rarity impacts:
                                                                                                                                             * Affix count (1/2/3/3/4)
                                                                                                                                             * Affix tier range (higher rarity can roll higher tier caps)
                                                                                                                                             * Salvage yield (better rarity returns more essence)
J6.2 Affix taxonomy (keep it consistent)
Affixes should fall into a few families so players can reason about them:
A) Core scaling
                                                                                                                                             * +Body, +Spirit, +Agility, +Will
B) Combat identity tags
                                                                                                                                             * “Sword Technique Damage +%”
                                                                                                                                             * “Fire Damage +%”
                                                                                                                                             * “Poison stacks +1”
                                                                                                                                             * “Shield on hit +X”
                                                                                                                                             * “Crit chance +% / Crit damage +%”
C) Utility / QoL (rare)
                                                                                                                                             * “Manual Pavilion refresh cost −%”
                                                                                                                                             * “Alchemy yield +%”
(Use sparingly or you’ll break economy.)
J6.3 Unique effects (Legendary only; 1 per item)
Example:
                                                                                                                                                * “When you apply Burn, gain 1 stack of Qi Surge (max 10). Each stack +1% technique potency.”
Uniques must be readable and must show up in combat event feed (Part C) or they won’t feel real.
J7. UX flows: equip, compare, lock, salvage
J7.1 Loot pickup → compare modal (mandatory)
Any new equipment drop triggers a small “New item” toast. Clicking it opens:
Compare Panel
Left: Equipped item
Right: New item
Middle: Deltas
                                                                                                                                                   * DPS: +8%
                                                                                                                                                   * EHP: −2%
                                                                                                                                                   * Build Tags: “Burn synergy +1 tier”
                                                                                                                                                   * “Net recommendation”: ✅ Upgrade / ⚠ Sidegrade / ❌ Downgrade
This is where you prevent 90% of “I don’t get it” confusion.
J7.2 Lock/Favorite (mandatory safety)
                                                                                                                                                   * Items can be Locked (cannot be salvaged/sold)
                                                                                                                                                   * Items can be Favorited (pins to top; used by loadout selection)
Batch actions must skip locked items without error.
J7.3 Salvage rules
                                                                                                                                                      * Salvage yields “Essence” (soft crafting currency) + “Shard” (rarity-based)
                                                                                                                                                      * Duplicate legendaries yield “Legend Fragment” used for re-rolls or upgrading unique effect (late-game)
This ties directly into Part G sink design.
J8. Gear upgrading: integrate with Forge and avoid system sprawl
You already have a Forge system (Part E). This Character/Status tab must make Forge upgrades feel obvious.
J8.1 Upgrade verbs (limit to 3)
                                                                                                                                                         1. Refine: linear +levels, increases base stats
                                                                                                                                                         2. Temper: rerolls 1 affix (costly; late-game)
                                                                                                                                                         3. Socket (optional): add rune slot (rare, gated)
J8.2 Upgrade CTA placement
Every item panel shows:
                                                                                                                                                         * “Refine to +2 (cost: …) [Go to Forge]”
                                                                                                                                                         * If insufficient resources: show missing ingredients with clickable sources (“Mine expedition”, “Bounty reward”, etc.)
J8.3 “Next best upgrades” uses a simple heuristic
                                                                                                                                                         * If weapon refine yields biggest DPS delta → show that
                                                                                                                                                         * If robe refine yields biggest EHP delta → show that
                                                                                                                                                         * If a technique upgrade yields more than either → show that
This makes the sheet feel smart without being intrusive.
J9. Loadouts (build slots)
J9.1 What a loadout includes
                                                                                                                                                            * Equipment set (per slot)
                                                                                                                                                            * Technique loadout (Part A’s Technique Library)
                                                                                                                                                            * Active Heart Law selection (Part B)
                                                                                                                                                            * Consumable policy (auto-use thresholds, Part D)
J9.2 Loadout switching rules
                                                                                                                                                            * Can switch only when not in combat and not in a foreground “locked state” activity
                                                                                                                                                            * Switching triggers recalculation + a “Build changed” summary toast
J9.3 Loadout export format (share + debugging)
Export: compressed JSON string (base64) containing item IDs and technique IDs only (no RNG seeds unless you choose to)
                                                                                                                                                            * Makes bug reports easy
                                                                                                                                                            * Supports “show your build” community behavior

Part K — Inventory, Stash, Codex, and Collection Systems Overhaul
This is the “make inventory not suck” section. If you get this wrong, your game becomes a junk-management sim and players churn.
K0. What “good” looks like
                                                                                                                                                            * The player can find any item in ≤10 seconds.
                                                                                                                                                            * The player can clean inventory in ≤15 seconds with batch actions.
                                                                                                                                                            * The player understands what to keep vs salvage.
                                                                                                                                                            * The player has a long-term “collector” motivation via Codex/Compendium without it becoming grind.
K1. Diagnosis: the three inventory killers
                                                                                                                                                            1. No filtering/sorting discipline → players drown.
                                                                                                                                                            2. No clear “actions” (lock/favorite/salvage) → players fear making mistakes.
                                                                                                                                                            3. No collection visibility → duplicates feel like insult, not progress.
Filter/sort confusion is a known UX problem; if you mix them or hide sorting controls, users underutilize them. (UXmatters)
K2. Inventory IA (tabs, not one giant bag)
Top-level Inventory tabs:
                                                                                                                                                            1. Equipment
                                                                                                                                                            2. Manuals (manual items before study; learned techniques live elsewhere)
                                                                                                                                                            3. Materials (alchemy/forge reagents)
                                                                                                                                                            4. Consumables (combat + cultivation)
                                                                                                                                                            5. Quest/Key Items (rare; mostly hidden from player unless needed)
Each tab shares the same structure:
                                                                                                                                                            * Top row: Search, Sort dropdown, Filter button
                                                                                                                                                            * Left rail (optional on desktop): quick filters (rarity, realm tier, type)
                                                                                                                                                            * Main: grid or list depending on item type
                                                                                                                                                            * Right: detail panel with actions
K3. Filtering and sorting rules (hard rules)
K3.1 Sort and filter must be distinct
                                                                                                                                                            * Sort = one dimension ordering (rarity, newest, power, refine level)
                                                                                                                                                            * Filter = constraints (rarity >= rare, slot=weapon, element=fire)
Mixing them causes mental model breaks; this is well documented in UX practice. (LogRocket Blog)
K3.2 Filtering must respect user intent
If the player selects multiple filters, do not reflow results aggressively mid-selection. Prefer:
                                                                                                                                                            * “Apply” button for multi-filter panels
                                                                                                                                                            * Or “intelligent filtering” behavior that waits until user is done. (Nielsen Norman Group)
K3.3 Saved filters
                                                                                                                                                            * “Salvage view” preset: shows unlocked, non-favorited, rarity <= rare
                                                                                                                                                            * “Upgrade candidates” preset: rarity >= epic, slot any
These are tiny features that massively reduce friction.
K4. Item actions (consistent and safe)
Actions must be consistent across all item types.
Universal actions
                                                                                                                                                               * Inspect (opens detail)
                                                                                                                                                               * Lock / Unlock
                                                                                                                                                               * Favorite / Unfavorite
                                                                                                                                                               * Move to Stash (if stash exists)
                                                                                                                                                               * Salvage (if salvageable)
                                                                                                                                                               * Sell (if vendor exists; optional)
Batch actions (must exist)
                                                                                                                                                               * “Select all shown”
                                                                                                                                                               * “Salvage selected”
                                                                                                                                                               * “Lock selected”
                                                                                                                                                               * “Move selected to stash”
Safety rules
                                                                                                                                                               * Locked items cannot be batch-salvaged
                                                                                                                                                               * Batch actions show a summary confirmation: “You will salvage 14 items (2 rares). Yield: …”
K5. Stash system (simple, expandable, not a second inventory)
Purpose: long-term storage + reduces decision pressure.
K5.1 Stash tabs
                                                                                                                                                               * Equipment Stash
                                                                                                                                                               * Materials Vault (optional; can be infinite if you want)
                                                                                                                                                               * Relics (late-game)
K5.2 Stash expansion
                                                                                                                                                               * Purchase with Merit or prestige currency (ties to Parts G and L)
                                                                                                                                                               * Expansion is a meaningful sink without being predatory.
K5.3 Auto-deposit rules
                                                                                                                                                               * Materials auto-stack and can auto-deposit to vault (toggle)
                                                                                                                                                               * Equipment never auto-deposits (avoid losing loot excitement)
K6. Codex/Compendium (turn “stuff” into “progress”)
This is your retention lever that is not pure monetization.
K6.1 What Codex tracks
                                                                                                                                                               * Items discovered (by definition ID, not instance)
                                                                                                                                                               * Techniques discovered (learned)
                                                                                                                                                               * Enemies encountered (bestiary)
                                                                                                                                                               * Herbs/ore discovered
                                                                                                                                                               * Recipes discovered
                                                                                                                                                               * City landmarks unlocked (optional)
K6.2 Why this works (and why it’s not optional)
Codex shifts the inventory experience from recall (“What did I see before?”) to recognition (“Oh, I’ve discovered 12/40 herbs here”). That is a direct usability win. (Nielsen Norman Group)
K6.3 Codex rewards (do not overdo)
Milestone rewards only, per category:
                                                                                                                                                               * Herbs: +Alchemy yield small permanent bonus
                                                                                                                                                               * Enemies: +Intel gain small permanent bonus
                                                                                                                                                               * Equipment: +Forge success rate small permanent bonus
Keep these bonuses modest; they’re retention texture, not primary progression.
K7. Inventory search (practical requirement)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Search must match: name, tags, element keywords, slot keywords

Part L — Prestige, Ascension, and Long-Term Meta Progression Overhaul
This maps to your top-level Prestige tab (Part F). This is where idle games live or die: a prestige reset must feel like “I became immortal” not “I threw away my progress.”
L0. Reference model: what prestige is supposed to do in idle games
Prestige in incremental/idle design is typically: reset most of the run’s transient progress in exchange for a persistent currency or multiplier that accelerates future runs (a “New Game+” loop). (Kongregate Newsroom)
Crucially, modern idle games also evolve prestige by adding variation between cycles (new powers, new goals), not only “faster numbers.” (Kongregate Newsroom)
L1. Overhaul decision: you need a 2-layer prestige model
If you implement only one prestige, you’ll run into either:
                                                                                                                                                                  * Too frequent resets → annoying
                                                                                                                                                                  * Too slow resets → boredom / “wall”
Recommendation:
                                                                                                                                                                  1. Ascension (frequent-ish): within a life, tied to realm breakthroughs (e.g., Earth → Heaven)
                                                                                                                                                                  2. Reincarnation (rare): full reset with major permanent unlocks
If you want to keep it simpler, implement only one now: Reincarnation as the main prestige. But design it so Ascension can be layered later.
L2. Terminology (player-facing)
Do not call it “Prestige” in the fiction. Use cultivation language.
                                                                                                                                                                  * Prestige (system term) → Reincarnation (player term)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Prestige currency → Heavenly Decree or Dao Marks
                                                                                                                                                                  * Prestige upgrades → Heavenly Boons / Dao Inscriptions
L3. What resets vs what persists (this is the trust contract)
This must be explicit in UI before confirming a reset.
Recommended persistence rules:
Persist (keep):
                                                                                                                                                                  * Codex discoveries (Part K)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Unlocked recipes (Alchemy/Forge)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Manual Pavilion shelf unlocks (not owned manuals)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Permanent “Heavenly Boons” upgrades
                                                                                                                                                                  * Cosmetic unlocks, UI QoL unlocks
                                                                                                                                                                  * Account achievements
Reset (lose):
                                                                                                                                                                  * City progression within the current life (re-earn fast)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Most equipment instances (or convert to a legacy currency)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Current gold/spirit stones (optional; consider partial carryover)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Active bounty board state (refresh)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Active expedition state (force resolve to avoid exploits)
Hybrid (partial):
                                                                                                                                                                  * Keep 1 “Heirloom” item (selected) per reincarnation (this feels amazing and reduces feel-bad)
                                                                                                                                                                  * Keep a percentage of materials (10–20%) as “reincarnation remnants” (optional)
The key is: the player must feel the reset is fair and purposeful.
L4. Reincarnation currency earn formula (simple, tunable, and explainable)
Earned currency should scale sublinearly with lifetime progress, so it’s valuable but not runaway.
A common pattern in idle prestige design is using logarithms or fractional exponents to “compress” large values into prestige currency. (Kongregate Newsroom)
Practical implementation (example):
                                                                                                                                                                  * Let LifetimeMerit be total Merit earned in a life
                                                                                                                                                                  * DaoMarks = floor( (LifetimeMerit / K) ^ 0.5 )
Where K is tuned so first reincarnation happens within a reasonable timeframe.
UI explanation (player-facing):
                                                                                                                                                                     * “Dao Marks are granted based on your lifetime deeds (Merit). Great deeds yield diminishing returns, encouraging timely reincarnation.”
L5. When should players reincarnate? (you must guide them)
Players will otherwise either:
                                                                                                                                                                     * Reincarnate too early and feel punished
                                                                                                                                                                     * Reincarnate too late and feel bored
Add a Reincarnation Advisor on the Prestige tab:
                                                                                                                                                                     * Shows current Dao Marks if you reincarnate now
                                                                                                                                                                     * Shows projected gain in the next milestone (e.g., “Next +1 Dao Mark at 2.3k Merit”)
                                                                                                                                                                     * Shows a “Worth it” badge (e.g., “Recommended when gain is meaningful”)
This mirrors established guidance patterns around prestige as a “reset for a boost,” but grounded in your currencies. (Kongregate Newsroom)
L6. Make prestige interesting: “choose a Dao Path each life”
This is how you prevent prestige from becoming only a speedrun.
On reincarnation, present a Dao Path choice (3 options early):
                                                                                                                                                                     * Sword Dao: +Sword technique bias, +crit scaling, reduced defense
                                                                                                                                                                     * Flame Dao: +Burn stacking and DoT, more volatile fights
                                                                                                                                                                     * Body Dao: +EHP and regen, slower kill speed
This creates run-to-run variation (a known innovation pattern in idle prestige loops). (Kongregate Newsroom)
L7. Prestige upgrade tree (Heavenly Boons)
Design as a tree with 5 categories so it stays legible:
A) Core Cultivation
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Qi gain multiplier
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Breakthrough stability
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Heart Law resonance effectiveness
B) Combat
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Technique mastery gain
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Boss drop chance
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Elite spawn rate (optional)
C) Economy
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Gold gain
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Merit gain
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Spirit stone gain
D) Professions
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Alchemy yield / quality
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Forge refine success
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Expedition rare drop chance
E) QoL
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Offline cap
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Auto-salvage rules unlocked
                                                                                                                                                                     * +Extra loadout slot
Progressive disclosure: show category summaries, expand into nodes. (Nielsen Norman Group)
L8. Prestige UI spec (top-level tab)
Left: Current Life Summary
                                                                                                                                                                     * Realm reached
                                                                                                                                                                     * Lifetime Merit / Spirit stones earned
                                                                                                                                                                     * Key achievements (boss clears, city milestones)
Center: Reincarnation Forecast
                                                                                                                                                                     * “You will gain: X Dao Marks”
                                                                                                                                                                     * “You will keep: …” list
                                                                                                                                                                     * “You will lose: …” list
                                                                                                                                                                     * Optional: choose 1 Heirloom
Right: Heavenly Boons
                                                                                                                                                                     * Spend Dao Marks
                                                                                                                                                                     * Tree visualization (simple vertical list early, full tree later)
Bottom: Confirm Reincarnation
                                                                                                                                                                     * Requires typing a confirmation word or holding button for 2 seconds (prevents misclick)

Part M — Accessibility, Localization, and In-Game Help Overhaul
This is the polish that prevents “the game feels janky/confusing” churn and reduces support load. Also: if you don’t internationalize early, you will regret it later.
M0. Accessibility target and scope
Target baseline: WCAG 2.2 AA for UI text and interaction where feasible. (W3C)
Do not treat this as “nice to have.” It directly improves usability for everyone, not only users with disabilities.
M1. Accessibility requirements (practical, implementable)
M1.1 Contrast
                                                                                                                                                                     * Normal text must meet minimum contrast ratios (AA). WCAG guidance commonly uses 4.5:1 for normal text and 3:1 for large text. (W3C)
Implementation:
                                                                                                                                                                     * Add a dev-only “contrast audit” page
                                                                                                                                                                     * Run automated checks (e.g., axe rules) during CI
Deque’s documentation summarizes how contrast checks map to WCAG thresholds. (Deque University)
M1.2 Keyboard navigation
                                                                                                                                                                        * All interactive elements reachable by Tab
                                                                                                                                                                        * Visible focus outlines
                                                                                                                                                                        * No keyboard traps
                                                                                                                                                                        * Modal dialogs trap focus appropriately and restore focus on close
M1.3 Screen reader and semantic structure
                                                                                                                                                                        * Use semantic HTML for headings/lists/buttons
                                                                                                                                                                        * Provide ARIA labels for icon-only buttons (e.g., lock/favorite)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Combat log and key state changes should use polite aria-live regions (so screen readers can announce important events without being spammy)
M1.4 Motion and effects
                                                                                                                                                                        * Add “Reduce Motion” toggle in Settings
                                                                                                                                                                        * Respect OS-level prefers-reduced-motion where possible
M2. Localization and internationalization (i18n) requirements
If you ever want to support multiple languages, you must stop hardcoding text now.
M2.1 String resources
                                                                                                                                                                        * All UI text lives in resource files (JSON)
                                                                                                                                                                        * No string concatenation in code for user-visible text; use message templates
M2.2 Language and direction metadata
W3C guidance emphasizes declaring language and base direction for strings and allowing defaults at document level (important for RTL languages). (W3C)
Practical approach:
                                                                                                                                                                        * Store strings with IDs
                                                                                                                                                                        * Each locale bundle includes metadata (lang + direction)
                                                                                                                                                                        * UI can switch direction for RTL locales
M2.3 Plurals, numbers, and time formatting
                                                                                                                                                                        * Use ICU-style message formatting (plurals, grammar, variables) to prevent “1 rooms” bugs and similar. (SimpleLocalize)
                                                                                                                                                                        * All numbers shown to players must be locale-formatted (decimal separators, grouping)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Dates/times must be consistently formatted (especially for “next refresh in …” timers). (Google for Developers)
M2.4 Text expansion
Some languages expand UI strings substantially; your buttons and cards must be flexible. This is a standard localization constraint; multiple localization best-practice guides emphasize planning for text expansion and not hardcoding layout. (gridly.com)
M3. In-game Help and Glossary (stop making players alt-tab)
You are building a cultivation game. Players will encounter genre terms and system terms. If you don’t provide a glossary, they will call the game confusing even if mechanics are fine.
M3.1 Glossary entries must exist for:
                                                                                                                                                                        * Qi
                                                                                                                                                                        * Dantian
                                                                                                                                                                        * Meridians
                                                                                                                                                                        * Heart Law (Xinfa)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Spirit Root
                                                                                                                                                                        * Technique vs Manual
                                                                                                                                                                        * Realm stages
For culturally rooted terms like “dantian,” you can ground definitions in common references: dantian is described as a “center of qi” in traditional Chinese medicine and related practices. (Wikipedia)
For meridians, you can describe them in-fiction as “channels through which qi flows,” reflecting how TCM conceptualizes them (while keeping it clearly in the game’s fantasy framing). (Wikipedia)
M3.2 Help system pattern
                                                                                                                                                                        * Every screen has a “?” icon that opens context help
                                                                                                                                                                        * Every resource has a tooltip in a consistent template (Part F already defines a good tooltip template)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Every complex system uses progressive disclosure: start with “what it is” + “why you care”, then “advanced”. (Nielsen Norman Group)
M4. Inventory/filters usability support
Because inventory is high-complexity, add a small “How to use filters” panel in Inventory with examples. This aligns with filter-design guidance about clarity and intent. (Nielsen Norman Group)
M5. Settings menu (must exist as its own top-level or in Character tab)
Minimum settings:
                                                                                                                                                                        * Text size (Small/Default/Large)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Reduce motion
                                                                                                                                                                        * Combat log verbosity (Minimal/Default/Verbose)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Number format (Suffix / Scientific / Full)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Language selector
                                                                                                                                                                        * Keybinds (optional)
M6. Acceptance criteria
                                                                                                                                                                        * Contrast meets WCAG baseline for key UI text. (W3C)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Keyboard navigation works across top-level tabs and modals.
                                                                                                                                                                        * Localization framework exists (resource bundles + formatting). (W3C)
                                                                                                                                                                        * Glossary exists and is reachable from tooltips/context help.
                                                                                                                                                                        * No hardcoded user-visible strings in new code added after this part.
